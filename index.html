I study how campaign communication, candidate characteristics, and political representation shape public opinion and democratic choice.

I am a doctoral candidate in American Politics and Statistical Methodology at Rutgers University. My work uses survey experiments, open-ended text, electoral returns, demographic data, and computational methods to answer questions about campaigns, representation, and voter behavior.

Current work

Negativity bias, representation, and AI-assisted survey research

My dissertation, Negativity Bias Revisited, examines how the effects of campaign appeals have changed over time. Related projects address candidate age, Latino voting, Puerto Rico’s status politics, and generative AI for classifying open-ended survey responses.
